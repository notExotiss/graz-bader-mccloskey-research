  FINDING: the single-node z-test evaluated only on NEIGHBOURING
  communities is selection-biased. From a singleton start, the mere
  existence of an edge is itself the rare event under the null
  (E[k_iB] ~ k_i*k_j/2m << 1), so almost every first move clears any
  fixed tau. Corollary 1's bound therefore does not hold for the
  literal single-node rule; suppression needs an initial partition or
  an all-pairs (not neighbour-only) candidate set. This is the kind of
  gap between the AI-drafted paper and its implementation that the
  Section 6 verification is designed to catch.
